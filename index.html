Hua Yang is a Ph.D. student in the Department of Computer Science at North Carolina State University, where he began his doctoral studies in August 2024 and expects to graduate in December 2027. He earned his M.S. in Computer Science from South China University of Technology and his B.S. in Electronic Engineering from Central South University.

His research sits at the intersection of trustworthy AI and software engineering, with a focus on securing large language models for code (LLMs4Code) from both the model and the data side. He studies three questions in particular: what training data these models memorize and leak (PII privacy risks), whether their use of restricted code can be detected or evaded (membership inference and code transformations), and how they can be aligned with regulatory requirements such as the GDPR. A recurring thread across this work is causal inference — moving past correlational evidence to establish why a model leaks what it leaks. This line of research has appeared at ICSE 2026.

Yang also works on AI infrastructure and agent evaluation. As a Software Engineer Intern at ByteDance (AI Infrastructure) in 2026, he built an offline evaluation framework for multi-turn on-call agents that converts production traces into causal graphs, together with a real-device execution harness for verifying agent-generated remediation workflows. Earlier, as a Machine Learning Intern at the AI Large Model for Intelligent Cognition Center, Pazhou Lab, he worked on trustworthy and interpretable AI, publishing at IEEE BIBM 2023 and in IEEE Transactions on Affective Computing.

He actively contributes to the research community as a reviewer for leading venues, including ICSE and IEEE Transactions on Affective Computing.

selected publications

  1. ICSE
    How Do Semantically Equivalent Code Transformations Impact Membership Inference on LLMs for Code?
    Hua Yang, Alejandro Velasco, Thanh Le-Cong, and 3 more authors
    In Proceedings of the 48th IEEE/ACM International Conference on Software Engineering (ICSE), 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    The success of Large Language Models for Code (LLMs4Code) relies on vast amounts of code data, including public open-source repositories and private, confidential company code, raising concerns about intellectual property compliance and the unauthorized use of license-restricted code. Membership inference (MI) has been proposed to detect such unauthorized usage, but its effectiveness may be undermined by semantically equivalent code transformation (SECT) rules. We systematically investigate how individual and combined SECT rules affect MI. Model accuracy drops by at most 1.5% per rule, showing that transformed datasets remain strong substitutes for fine-tuning, while the RenameVariable rule reduces MI success by up to 10.19%. Causal analysis confirms that variable renaming has the strongest causal effect in disrupting MI detection.

  2. arXiv
    Understanding Privacy Risks in Code Models Through Training Dynamics: A Causal Approach
    Hua Yang, Alejandro Velasco, Sen Fang, and 2 more authors
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2512.07814, 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Large Language Models for Code are trained on open-source repositories that contain abundant Personally Identifiable Information (PII), which can be memorized during training and resurfaced at inference time. Prior work has largely treated PII as a homogeneous category, overlooking the heterogeneous risks posed by different PII types. We build a real-world dataset of 9,000 code files spanning six PII types, fine-tune six code LLMs (3B-14B) across the Stable Code, CodeLlama, and Qwen2.5-Coder families, compute training dynamics on real PII data, and formulate a structural causal model to estimate the causal effect of learning difficulty on leakage. Results show that easy-to-learn instances drive leakage while ambiguity plays a more complex, type- and scale-dependent role, providing the first causal evidence for designing type-aware and learnability-aware defenses.
